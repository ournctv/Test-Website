About the Nantucket Civic League:

The mission of the Nantucket Civic League, founded in 1903 and representing about 24 neighborhood associations and over 1800 dues-paying families, is to promote the general welfare of Nantucket through informed citizen participation in community affairs.

Civic Engagement

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Pauline Proch

Pauline Proch describes how the school population has shifted — from roughly 90 percent white students when she started to about half today, with nearly 40 percent of children speaking a language other than English as their first language. She argues that a school committee should reflect the community it serves, and encourages families of color and parents of younger children to run or to join school councils as parent representatives.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Brooke Mohr

Brooke Mohr, vice chair of the Affordable Housing Trust and chair of the Council for Human Services, describes how much more readily one can affect change on Nantucket than in a larger community. Her advice for anyone curious is to start by attending public meetings and speaking during the public comment portion, then to take a back seat and listen for a while after joining a committee.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Maria Partida

Maria Partida came to the island at nineteen and started out cleaning houses, then found herself interpreting for neighbors at the hospital, at school, and in job interviews. Asked why she volunteers so much, her answer is simply “why not” — and she describes the people serving on island committees as willing to help and willing to find solutions.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Ray Pohl

Ray Pohl, an architect who moved to Nantucket in 1990, chairs the Historic District Commission after seven years on the board. He explains what the commission is actually charged with — regulating design so that new building keeps faith with the architectural traditions established in the whaling days — and corrects a common misreading of that mission as limiting growth.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Denice Kronau

Denice Kronau came to the Finance Committee by way of the group founded after the 2016 election to get Nantucket women engaged in civic activity. A former chief financial officer, she put together a résumé and petitioned each member of the Select Board for the appointment, then spent her first year mostly listening — on the principle that one of her key skills is being able to say she doesn’t know and ask for help.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Rita Higgins

Rita Higgins first came to Nantucket as a small child, returned after university, and settled on the island full time in 2013. With a background in the environment, she describes the fine balance between the island’s history, its architecture, and the wildness of the moors and dunes — and why, in 2017, she came to feel that balance needed someone advocating for it.

N. Civic League’s Interviews about Civic Engagement: Vanessa Rendeiro

Vanessa Rendeiro, a former English and Portuguese teacher from Belém in Brazil, has lived on Nantucket for about twelve years. Her interpreting work began early in the pandemic when she was asked to help translate hospital announcements for the Brazilian community; she now assists both the Brazilian and Hispanic communities daily, and was appointed to her position by a vote of the Select Board.

Meet the Articles – October 25, 2025

The November 4 Special Town Meeting was called by citizens’ petition — the first in roughly forty years — and is devoted entirely to short-term rentals. Co-president Scott Wilson lays out the split: Article 1 would codify renting as a primary right across the island, while Article 2, from the Planning Board, would make it an accessory right with added restrictions — a division that left the Planning Board 3–2 for Article 2, the Finance Committee 4–3 for Article 1, and the Select Board deadlocked 2–2.

Investing In Our Future: Extending the Sewer System – 3/5/16

A forum on the proposed expansion of Nantucket’s sewer system, focused on articles 16 through 19 on the warrant for the April 2 Annual Town Meeting. Town representatives explain how nitrogen from septic systems affects water quality in the harbor and ponds, and why the adaptive management plan the EPA requires under the Clean Water Act means expanding public infrastructure. Finance director Brian Turbitt then sets out what the work would cost and how it would be paid for — betterments on the properties served, and borrowing through the State Revolving Fund.

Introducing the New Nantucket Cottage Hospital – 5/21/16

A forum introducing the new Nantucket Cottage Hospital, moderated by the hospital’s public information officer, Jason Graziadei. President and CEO Dr. Margot Hartmann explains why the 1957 building can no longer support the way care is delivered — one operating room that doubles as the procedure room, and only the CT scanner room meeting current size standards — and the hospital’s counsel, clinical program director, and president of the medical staff walk through the permitting process, the interior layout, and how the new building will go up around the existing one while it keeps running.

Nantucket Civic League: In-Town Parking Concerns – 2/18/17

Dave Fredericks, for the Harbor Place project, makes the case for a transportation center on the old coal gasification site — not, he insists, simply a parking garage — and town transportation planner Mike Burns sets out the three measures recommended to the Select Board: paid parking downtown, more capacity through a parking structure or satellite lots with park-and-ride, and expanded year-round transit. Select Board member Matt Fee gives the board’s view, Peter Morrison reports early results from a pilot survey of residents, and moderator Charlie Stott takes questions from the floor.

Town Hall

Town Hall of Nantucket with Senator Markey 8/10/17

Senator Ed Markey takes questions at the Atheneum on the fight over the Affordable Care Act, days after the repeal bill’s defeat, and on the opioid and heroin epidemic that killed some 2,000 people in Massachusetts in 2016 — arguing that the test of any commitment is treatment funding rather than rhetoric. He makes the case for a clean energy build-out as both a climate necessity and a jobs opportunity, and answers a question on school funding and whether money for charter schools should come out of existing public school budgets. State Representative Dylan Fernandes hosts, alongside the state treasurer.

Nantucket Civic League – State Rep Public Meeting – 10/10/17

State Senator Julian Cyr and State Representative Dylan Fernandes report on the affordable housing bank bill, which has cleared two committees and sits in the last one before engrossment, and on deepwater offshore wind as the state’s clearest competitive advantage in clean energy. They take questions on prevention spending in the opioid and heroin epidemic — Barnstable County puts under two percent of its funding toward it — on the Safe Communities Act and how little authority a state has over immigration, and on school enrollment, where Nantucket is the only district either represents that is still growing.

Rep. Bill Keating (US House, MA 9th District) Nantucket Virtual Town Hall – 10/20/20

Congressman Bill Keating answers island questions on the relief package then under negotiation, where child care tax credits and funding are among the sticking points, and on the affordable housing written into the infrastructure bill. He turns to problems particular to Nantucket: nearly $20 million for airport infrastructure and a new taxiway at what is the second-busiest airport in Massachusetts after Logan, and the CARES Act money the Steamship Authority was able to reach once its service was reclassified as commuter ferry service. Asked about the coming election, he cites the FBI director’s testimony that the greatest terror threat is now domestic.

Nantucket Town Hall with Sen. Cyr and Rep. Moakley

State Senator Julian Cyr and State Representative Thomas Moakley take questions from islanders on what Nantucket wants from Beacon Hill. Much of the evening goes to the real estate transfer fee the town has long sought on high-value sales to fund affordable housing — Cyr calls it his first priority — along with the Steamship Authority’s leadership transition and keeping the line island-controlled, and the region’s multi-billion-dollar wastewater problem, now partly paid for by the Cape Cod and Islands Water Protection Fund. They also field questions on last winter’s steep electricity bills and smart meters, and on state support for public schools as federal funding changes.
