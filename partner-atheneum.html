About the Nantucket Atheneum:

We are Nantucket’s free public library and gathering place, transforming lives with resources and experiences that support lifelong learning for all.

The island’s public library is a vital community hub. We are open year-round, 6 days a week, and offer materials and services for all ages.

In the last year, the Atheneum attracted over 65,000 visits and 25,000 human experiences of the library’s educational and cultural programming. The Atheneum lent from its diverse collections, over 125,000 times.

Our historic building, which opened in 1847, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and filled with beautiful art and artifacts that celebrate our maritime heritage and the history of Nantucket.

2019 Geschke Lecture Series: Hon. Kevin Rudd, US – China Relations: The Avoidable War

Kevin Rudd, former Prime Minister of Australia and a Mandarin-speaking China scholar, closes the summer’s Geschke Lecture Series by arguing that conflict between the United States and China is not inevitable. He lays out China’s grand strategy as its own leadership sees it — seven concentric priorities beginning with keeping the Communist Party in power and extending outward to reshaping the global rules-based order — then assesses how well that strategy is actually working, from Hong Kong and Taiwan to a slowing economy and a transformed relationship with Russia. Rudd closes with what he believes an effective American response would require: an actual China strategy rather than an attitude, sustained investment in the sources of national power, far greater reliance on allies, and a bipartisan consensus durable enough to outlast individual administrations.

2021 Geschke Lecture Series: John Grisham & Dr. Neal Kassell, Curing With Sound

Best-selling novelist John Grisham and neurosurgeon Neal Kassell, MD, team up for a conversation about focused ultrasound, an early-stage, noninvasive medical technology that has the potential to irrevocably change medicine.

Once called medicine’s “best-kept secret,” the technology is now FDA-approved for treatment of Parkinson’s disease tremors, essential tremor, prostate cancer, bone metastases, osteoid osteomas, and uterine fibroids. Kassell and Grisham are optimistic that it can address a wide range of other diseases as well. Clinical trials are ongoing globally, with an emphasis on brain disorders (including Alzheimer’s and brain tumors), cancer, and immunotherapy.

Join us for a fascinating conversation that asks us to imagine a new way to think about and treat major disease, without invasive surgery or radiation.

2022 Geschke Lecture Series: Tracy Edwards

Tracy Edwards recounts how a teenager expelled from school at fifteen became the skipper of Maiden and the first all-female crew to race around the world. She traces the path from a backpacking trip to Greece and a job on a charter yacht, through the 1985-86 Whitbread — where four of 260 crew were women — to remortgaging her house to buy a secondhand boat, rebuilding it by hand, and finding a sponsor in King Hussein of Jordan after journalists bet the crew wouldn’t finish the first leg. Maiden went on to win two legs in the Southern Ocean and take second overall, still the best British result since 1977. Edwards closes on the boat’s second life: rescued from a marina in the Seychelles in 2014, restored, and now sailing the world raising funds for girls’ education.

2022 Geschke Lecture Series: The New England Aquarium

Vikki Spruill, president and CEO of the New England Aquarium, sits down with chief scientist Dr. John Mandelman and senior scientist Dr. Nick Whitney for a conversation about sharks in Nantucket waters. The three discuss why sightings have risen so sharply — a mix of recovering white shark and seal populations, abundant forage fish, a rapidly warming Gulf of Maine, and the simple fact that everyone now carries a camera — and identify roughly fifteen shark species found around the island, most of them far less familiar than the white shark. Mandelman and Whitney describe the Aquarium’s research methods, from acoustic “shark EZ-Pass” receivers and accelerometer tags that log a shark’s every tail beat, to the sea turtle hospital and the right whale catalog, and close on what that science makes possible: you cannot protect what you cannot find.

About Angela Stent
Dr. Stent is one of the nation’s leading experts on Russia, its foreign policy, and relations with the United States. Her newest book, Putin’s World, is described by William Burns, current CIA director and former Deputy Secretary of State, as “the definitive guide to understanding the tangled history of post-Cold War Russia…could not be more timely.”

Dr. Stent is currently Senior Non-Resident Fellow at the Brookings Institution where she co-chairs the Hewitt Forum on Post-Soviet Affairs. She formerly directed the Center on Russian, Eurasian, and East European Studies at Georgetown University where she was Professor of Government and Foreign Service. She has served as National Intelligence Officer for Russia and Eurasia at the National Intelligence Council. She also served in the Office of Policy Planning at the U.S. Department of State and on the senior advisory panel for NATO’s Supreme Allied Commander in Europe.

Her latest book Putin’s World won the Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy’s prize for the best book on U.S.-Russian Relations. Dr. Yergin is a highly respected authority on energy, international politics, and economics, and a Pulitzer Prize winner. Time Magazine said, “If there is one man whose opinion matters more than any other on global energy markets, it’s Daniel Yergin.” The New York Times described Daniel Yergin as “America’s most influential energy pundit.” Dr. Yergin is Vice Chairman of S&P Global.

Dr. Yergin’s new book The New Map: Energy, Climate and the Clash of Nations is described by NPR as “a master class on how the world works”, by the London Sunday Times as a “wonderful book” and, in The Washington Post, as “a tour de force of geopolitical understanding.” It is a Wall Street Journal bestseller.

Dr. Yergin is the author of the bestseller The Quest: Energy, Security, and the Remaking of the Modern World. His book The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil Money and Power was awarded the Pulitzer Prize, was a number one New York Times bestseller, and has been translated into 20 languages. He co-authored Commanding Heights: The Battle for the World Economy, of which the Wall Street Journal said “No one could ask for a better account of the world’s political and economic destiny since World War II.” Both The Prize and The Commanding Heights were made into multi-hour television series for PBS and BBC.

He is a board member of the Council on Foreign Relations and a senior trustee of the Brookings Institution.

2024 Geschke Lecture Series: Dr. Jonathan Koppell

Dr. Jonathan Koppell: As President of Montclair State University, Koppell advocates for the role of universities as engines of public good that bring enormous value to their communities and the nation. He demonstrates the importance of fostering deep community partnerships, and illustrates the role that higher ed plays in ensuring a diverse leadership pipeline in America. Montclair State University is New Jersey’s second largest higher education institution. This commitment was exemplified by Koppell’s decision to intervene when Bloomfield College, New Jersey’s only Predominantly-Black Institution, faced imminent closure. Montclair successfully engineered Bloomfield’s stabilization and merger into Montclair and is now establishing a model for a differentiated liberal arts college within a public university.

He serves on the boards of several nonprofit organizations and has been a visiting scholar at the Brookings Institution, a Markle Fellow at the New America Foundation and a Fulbright Lecturer in Shanghai, China.

This historical fiction is set in the late 1800s in Paris and profiles two sisters that pursue salvation from their financial trouble in ballet and the theater. They come to realize that their true salvation, however, is with each other and not 19th century French high-society.

2022 Nantucket Annual Summer Forum

Nantucket’s 15th Annual Summer Forum was held on July 18, 2022. The event was co-sponsored by the Nantucket Community Association and, for the first time, the Nantucket Civic League. The Forum provides the public, in particular our summer residents and visitors, an opportunity to listen to and to speak with local governmental leaders about important island issues. Participants included members of the Select Board, the Town Manager, and Town Department Heads.

One Book One Island: Yummy Monday: Seafood and Andouille Gumbo

In honor of Nantucket’s One Book One Island selection The Yellow House by Sarah Broom, we’re doing a Creole themed Yummy Monday.

Ron Bocage will demonstrate how to make a New Orleans classic, Seafood & Andouille Gumbo, using ingredients available on Nantucket.

Ron and his wife Myrna were born and raised in New Orleans and have been visiting Nantucket since 1973. They moved to Nantucket upon retirement in 2007.
